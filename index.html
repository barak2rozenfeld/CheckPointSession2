Web mix cites NetSecOpen traffic and the Firewall, Application Control and IPS blend. The headline HTTP/TLS row references the chart’s proprietary service blends. Preserve the source footnotes when comparing.

Illustrative HQ requirements

One active/standby pair. Peak 4 Gbps now, planning case 8 Gbps at month 24. A 25% margin gives acceptance loads of 5 and 10 Gbps.

70% of traffic volume requires TLS inspection. At 5 Gbps, that is 3.5 Gbps of inspected encrypted traffic within the total workload. The other 30% still receives applicable policy enforcement.

Traffic growth, inspection ratio and margin are case assumptions. They are not market statistics or a capacity guarantee.

Shortlist, then qualification

9700 is the initial evaluation candidate. 9800 is compared for measured workload or connectivity benefit. 9400 can be considered if it meets all requirements and lifecycle cost is better.

Do not interpolate capacity linearly from the 70% ratio. Validate TLS versions, ciphers, object sizes, connection rate, concurrency and all required services.

Available growth must stand alone

Quote the initial pair, management, subscriptions and support together with a qualified future design. Compare a larger platform upfront with a staged larger-platform or Maestro deployment.

HTTP/TLS figures are not interchangeable with web-mix results. Do not assume the 9800 alone passes the 10 Gbps customer workload. The proposed module is excluded from the base capacity plan.

A3

Module feasibility and engineering gates

The customer promise depends on a supported, secure upgrade implementation.

Hardware and existing appliances

Confirm module fit in the intended 9700/9800 I/O design, host bandwidth, power, cooling, firmware and remaining ports. Neither a generic PCIe card fit nor field-upgrade compatibility is established.

Retrofitting the installed base is a gate, not a proven fact. Failure here changes both the product proposition and the Session 1 business model.

[9] TLS processing

NVIDIA kTLS documentation describes TLS record crypto offload. Handshake remains in user space and unsupported processing falls back to software.

A gateway inspection proxy still needs integration of both TLS legs, session state, supported ciphers, error paths and security inspection. kTLS alone does not prove the proposed full gateway performance.

[11] Pattern matching and support

NVIDIA forum response, Dec. 11, 2025 reports discontinued DOCA RegEx/DPI library support and points to legacy DPDK RegEx on suitable hardware, including BF3.

Obtain a supported software and maintenance path before committing scope or schedule. Measure signature eligibility, parsing, false positives and software fallback. Hardware presence alone is insufficient.

Security, resilience and economics

Require equivalent policy outcomes, logs and coverage with acceleration on or off. Test module failure, HA state, recovery and surviving-member throughput.

Session 1’s 25 Gbps+ TP with TLS gate is an unmeasured engineering target. Its 40 Gbps ambition is not a customer sizing claim. Use identical baseline and target profiles. Retention and revenue forecasts remain conditional.

Proposed scope only. Validate vendor support, technical fit and customer economics before setting price, launch date or an upgrade commitment.

A5

Evaluation detail and migration readiness

Agree the workload, protections and pass/fail thresholds before equipment arrives.

Workload and security

Record the traffic mix, peak rate, new connections per second, concurrent sessions, object sizes, packet sizes and session duration. Define both traffic directions.

Test the required prevention services, signatures and update version. Include benign traffic, false positives and log completeness. Do not infer efficacy from throughput alone.

TLS and application compatibility

Agree TLS versions and ciphers, certificate trust distribution, pinned applications, mutual TLS, privacy exclusions and QUIC/HTTP/3 policy.

Measure the fraction actually inspected. Explicitly record bypassed traffic. Confirm that excluded traffic receives the remaining applicable controls.

Resilience and growth

Define p95 latency, recovery time and allowable session loss numerically. Exercise failover and failback at 5 Gbps, then qualify the selected future topology at 10 Gbps under its agreed failure case.

Include management reachability, logs, synchronization, link failures and enough soak time for representative peaks. Required future equipment is separately scoped.

Migration and handover

Review objects, rules, NAT, routing, identity, VPN and log destinations. Test certificate distribution and approved application exceptions before cutover.

Agree a maintenance window, rollback trigger, owners and escalation path. Rehearse on representative configurations. Approval requires security, network, application and procurement sign-off.

Evaluation design assumptions. Proposed two-week execution window starts only after environment and equipment readiness. Extend if the agreed scope requires it.
